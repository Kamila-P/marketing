# Handy Checklist
Step-by-step check-up, see where you're at with your project's marketing and what you can improve. Quickly. If this seems super basic - good. You want to have this covered before you spend more time on your marketing skills. 
## Is it clear what it is?
1. **Keep it simple** If reader is confused about what your thing does, who is it for and what do you want them to do with it, you lost them already. Leave all the *"leveraging synergy for a better alignment"* bs out. Just say it as it is.
2. **Have a clear CTA** Meaning Call to Action. What do you want from people? Are you looking for testers? Users? Contributors? Sponsors? People need to know what your project **is**, what it **does** and what do you want them to **do** with it.
### Good examples:
**Lustre** "A Gleam web framework for building HTML templates, single page applications, and real-time server components."

**Pushin** "European Git hosting. Git hosting that never leaves Europe. Explore repos/Start using Pushin" 

**Atom VM** "AtomVM lets you build with Erlang, Elixir and Gleam for microcontrollers, IoT systems and WebAssembly-powered frontends, using familiar tools and a modern concurrent programming model."

## Can people find it?
1. **Is the name consistent across different channels?** You might want to be creative with the way you talk about your project, but make sure the main name doesn't change. Check the spelling too.
2. **Do all your profiles have a link to your website or repo?** If I visit your bsky profile, discord bio, linkedin page, will I be able to access it in one click? Or is it hidden? Or not there at all? Check the spelling too. Really, go check it.
3. **Is the name unique?** No worries if it's not, Elixir and Gleam are also common nouns but people can still find both. If that's the case for your project, make sure you have a consistent follow-up word. For these it would be "lang" or "language", you can try adding a short tagline clarifying what your project is about.
## Are you talking about it enough?
1. **How many channels are you using?** Not every project needs a separate profile or page, but you need to reach out to people. Consider creating your own list of places where you can post news about your thing. **Scroll down for some ideas.** Diversifying channels helps you with testing your messaging (how you word things, what arguments or examples you use) and reaching out to different crowds.
2. **Is it added to your other profiles?** If you have a separate account (e.g. on LinkedIn or bsky) for the project, don't forget to also mention it on your main or private ones. For small projects, there's a high chance you as a person have more followers and contacts than your project does, make sure they can easily find your other thing. For bigger projects, consider creating a profile for it as a "company" on linkedin, so you can list it on your career experience history. Adding it as a company makes it clickable (clickable is good). You can add it to your email footer or as a slack status. Don't be shy!
3. **Are you collecting social proofs?** GitHub stars, opinions, case studies, even screenshots of nice comments are valuable validation. Bragging is highly encouraged. Remember: Promotion isn’t vanity. It’s survival.
## Are you up to date?
1. **Keep one source of truth** Big gaps between posts or confusing lack of updates happen to everyone. Make sure whoever wants to get fresh info on your project knows which channels are up to date and which are abandoned. If you're done with a platform for whatever reason and you don't want to delete the profile, just pin a post about where people can find you now.
2. **Unpin that old shit** Check if the first thing visitors see is still relevant. Maybe you forgot to unpin an invitation to a meetup from few months ago. Or maybe the top featured post is about v1.0 release and you're on v16.1 already.
3. **Update threads too** If you have your little space in the community where you used to talk about your project (as a discord thread/post, slack channel, forum topic), post the updates there as well. People participating in the conversation are already interested in it! If you don't have time for it, tell them where to find the latest info (it should be your one source of truth, you know that). 
# Where to promote it?
This is a non-exhaustive list of places where you can promote your thing. **Make sure to always check specific rules** on what can be posted, in what format, what channel etc. No, saying "Kamila told me to" is not a universal get-out-of-jail card, sorry. 

## Elixir and BEAM specific
- **Elixir Status** https://elixirstatus.com
Add your project update, event invitation, blog post. Selected entries are included in the Elixir Weekly newsletter.

- **Elixir Slack(s)** https://elixir-slack.community
There’s the official one, but you can also post about Elixir stuff on Erlang Ecosystem Foundation 

- **Elixir Discord(s)** https://discord.com/invite/elixir
Same as slack: there's the main, official one, but many others you can use to post. Look into local ones (Berlin, FR, Africa) or for a more narrowed down topics (Ash, Tidewave) as well. Don't forget to use conferences discords to share your stuff.
- **Elixir Reddit** https://www.reddit.com/r/elixir/
- **Other Discords and Slacks**
Elixir is popular enough to have its own channels on other servers. Check out the ones about functional programming, maybe some local ones.
- **Elixir Forum** https://elixirforum.com
I don't think it needs any explanation
- **Elixir Linkedin Groups**
It's a thing, but these aren't really active, so you can test if it works for you.
- **Virtual event spaces** Like event discords and apps
Nowadays most conferences use some sort of app for people to communicate before, during and after the event. If you're at Goatmire, make use of that space! 

- **Talks and podcasts**
BEAM and Elixir podcasts want to have cool stories and guests that have built something interesting. Check out their info about submitting your topic proposal.
Same goes for conferences and local/virtual meetups. If you're not ready for a big thing, start with a lightning talk!
- **EEF registry** https://registry.erlef.org/registry
Add your company!

## Not-BEAM
- **Lobsters** https://lobste.rs/t/elixir
A general techy post aggregator, not for every update, use it for bigger content or some mayor updates/releases. Read the rules of posting. You need invitation to post and the user that invited you is visible on your profile, that helps to keep spammers out.
- **Hacker News** https://news.ycombinator.com
Also for bigger content pieces. Once you're on that podcast, gave the talk or written a blog, this is where you go to brag.
- **Your work** Really.
I mean, unless you're self-employed. If you work somewhere, your marketing and HR team want you to share your side project. It makes them look cooler. They get cute "look at our happy and passionate employees" content, you get their reach and channels. 

### How to choose where to post?** 
As always, it depends. 
For most projects, it's important to reach people outside of the Elixir community. Browse the channels on discords and forums where you're already active, maybe they have a place where it would be fitting to talk about your thing? 

**Try looking for the best fit based on:**
- **technical angle**: a DevOps tool can easily be shared on a DevOps discord, if you're combining Elixir+Gleam, go brag in Gleam spaces etc.
- **use case**: post where it's relevant to end users. You've created a small, daily puzzle style game? Like [hashi](https://hashi.giacomocavalieri.me/)? Share it where people are looking for something to add to their list with wordle, linkedin  games, Clues by Sam and others.
- **industry/topic**: there's a high chance you can find people interested in your project that don't give a damn about the tech behind it. If your project is not a dev2dev thing, adjust the communication about it and focus on the end user.
